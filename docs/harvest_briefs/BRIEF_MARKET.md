3. Why this round exists: prices, trades and market data do NOT contain WAT. What the commercial and financial world publishes are crude QUALITY documents. Look for those: assays, certificates of quality, tender and sale specifications, quality bank and pipeline specifications, competent person reports, prospectuses, annual reports, environmental and field development documents. A source counts only if it states a measured WAT (or wax appearance point, wax precipitation temperature, cloud point with the method named), or at least wax content AND pour point with density or API for a crude that has a WAT elsewhere. Price series, cargo volumes, freight and customs volumes are out of scope. Do not extract them. If you find a document that links a price discount or penalty to wax, pour point or WAT, log it in log.csv with status extracted or no_wat and describe the link in reason; that is thesis context.
4. Search budget: previous agents ran out of WebSearch calls midway. Spend searches carefully: about 30 well-aimed searches, then use WebFetch on listing pages, indexes and document links. Prefer index pages that list many assays or reports over single-document hits. Use standard mode first.
5. Public documents only. No logins, no paywalls, no terms-of-service bypass, no mirrors, caches, proxies or Tor. If a document needs an account or a payment, log it as unreachable and move on. Do not enter any personal data anywhere.
6. Tier guide for commercial documents: a laboratory certificate or assay with named standard methods and all four inputs is tier A. A company assay with a WAT but no named methods is A-. A trade-press or prospectus summary of an assay is B- or C. A bare number in a news item is tier P at best.
