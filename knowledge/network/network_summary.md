## Where more data hides (plain reading of the network)

1. Kok and the METU group. Kok 1996 (15 crudes, WAT by DSC, microscopy and viscometry), Letoffe et al. 1995 (14 oils, DSC and thermomicroscopy), Kok 1999 "DSC and rheometry investigations of crude oils" and Kok 2007 "Comparative methods in the determination of wax content and pour points" all have green open copies in the METU repository (hdl.handle.net/11511/41175, /49045, /41616, /46456). Earlier agents logged them as unreachable through other URLs. This is the best single cluster to open next: same lab, same methods, many oils with API, pour point and wax content.
2. The cooling-rate and method-comparison cluster. Japper-Jaafar 2016 (DSC vs thermomicroscopy vs rheometry, cooling rate) is the most cited WAT measurement paper not in master but is closed. Its companion, "Non-linear dependency of WAT on cooling rate" (2018), has a green NTNU copy (hdl.handle.net/11250/2563250). Also open: Modulated DSC for wax crystallization (Fuel 2017, bronze PDF) and RGB image analysis WAT (Fuel 2018, bronze PDF).
3. WDT models all lean on the same small set of high-pressure or model-mixture data (Daridon and Coutinho group: Vieira 2007 high-pressure setup, Pauly 2002 synthetic waxes, tetradecane binaries 2005, Chevallier 1999 ternaries, Dauphin 1999 and 2001 partially frozen systems). These are mixtures, not dead crude oils, so they are of limited direct use for our bulk-property model, but they explain why the Benamara 2019, Bian 2019, Amiri-Ramsheh 2022 and Nait Amar 2024 sets overlap so much (Jaccard 0.32 to 0.50) and why they should be treated as one family, not independent evidence.
4. The WAT ML papers (Benamara 2020, Kouhi 2025, Yadav 2025) do not share a dataset with the WDT family. Kouhi and Yadav overlap strongly (9 shared candidate sources, Jaccard 0.35): both cite Kok 1996, Kok 2018 (different-origin crudes by DSC), Alcazar-Vara 2011 (Mexican crudes), Japper-Jaafar 2016, the Iranian WAT papers (2018 wax-composition study and 2019 ultrasonic-wave study), the 2003 DSC wax-content paper, the 2014 ultrasonic reservoir-condition WAT paper (gold OA) and Benamara 2020 itself. This is the likely backbone of their 81-point set and of Yadav's set. Benamara 2020 uses its own 59 Algerian oils (already in master).
5. Mexican and Iranian crude sets (Alcazar-Vara 2011; the Iranian WAT series 2017 to 2019; Hosseinipour CO2 2016) are cited by many WAT papers but are closed. Author repositories or ResearchGate requests are the only route.
6. Theses: the network surfaces open theses from Chulalongkorn (Fang and Mae Soon fields), Unicamp and PUC-Rio (Brazilian waxy crudes), theses.fr (paraffins under pressure, 2014) and a UNH dissertation on acoustic detection of crude oil properties. Brazilian theses often carry full crude property tables.

## Caveats

- trains_on edges are reference-list signals, not checked against the ML papers' data tables. Treat them as "probably uses data from".
- "likely open" OA status is a guess from the publisher DOI prefix. "(OpenAlex)" status was checked on 2026-11-05.
- Edges from master_v9 (measures, uses_method, reports) come straight from the master rows. Title-keyword method and property edges for OpenAlex works are rough.
- Seeds without a DOI (assays, tenders, theses, regulator reports, search pages) are nodes in the network, but they have no citation edges.
- Citing-work lists for the thermodynamic core papers (Lira-Galeana 280, Coutinho 1998 134) and for the smaller ML papers were not pulled. This is the main gap. Next step: fetch those lists, and fetch reference lists for the 29 open candidates once they are opened.
